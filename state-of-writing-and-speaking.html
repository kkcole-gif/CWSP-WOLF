The State of Writing and Speaking at NC State

Campus findings from the Campus Writing and Speaking Program, fall 2026, on student learning outcomes filed by July 2026.

The Campus Writing and Speaking Program (CWSP), housed in the Office of the Provost, supports the teaching of writing and speaking in every NC State college. In fall 2026, CWSP read every student learning outcome the university's degree and certificate programs had filed with its assessment system by July 2026, at every credential level from associate degrees and undergraduate certificates through doctorates. Because accreditors and the university's own assessment process read a program's teaching through these outcome statements, communication a program teaches and assesses without naming it in an outcome stays out of the institutional record. The campus figures follow, with the pattern for each college, and CWSP invites departments whose outcomes do not yet name the communication their faculty assess to work through the record with us.

On this page, a program documents communication when at least one of its filed outcomes names a communicative act, such as communicate, write, speak, present, argue, or address an audience. The measure is the written record, and on its own it says nothing about how much writing and speaking a program's students do.

How CWSP read the record

CWSP's 2025 study read the outcomes undergraduate programs published in the university catalog, under a reporting framework the university has since replaced. The 2026 reading covers every outcome statement the 336 programs filed with the assessment system by July 2026, so the two studies are not compared, and these figures serve as the baseline for the next assessment cycle.

CWSP coded each statement under a tiered codebook, counting a program as documenting communication when at least one of its outcomes names a communicative act: communicate, write, speak, present, argue, articulate, address an audience, or compose in visual and multimodal forms. Statements naming a product without an act (a thesis, a paper, a publication) were recorded separately, as were statements naming only cognition (analyze, interpret, explain). Because 337 of the 2,540 statements are graduate satisfaction items (such as "be satisfied with the overall educational experience of the certificate program") or statements of what faculty expect of the program, every share of outcomes reported here uses the 2,203 learning outcomes as its denominator.

What 336 programs document

Two hundred thirty-three programs, 69.3 percent of the 336, carry at least one outcome naming communication, a majority at every credential level above the certificate. Of the remaining 103 programs, 79 have outcome text with no reference to communication, ten name a communicative product without the act, ten have no outcome text on file, three report only satisfaction items, and one names communication in a program objective without carrying it into an outcome.

At the level of the statement, 495 outcomes name communication, 22.5 percent of learning outcomes. Programs rely on a general vocabulary, in which communicate appears in 256 statements across 183 programs, write in 136, oral in 118, and presentation in 114. Sixty-eight statements specify an audience, and the disciplinary terms of rhetoric and composition (argue, rhetoric, compose, and speak) appear, taken together, in fewer than twenty statements across the university.

Why documentation varies

Doctoral programs name communication almost without exception, because publication, presentation, and defense are the terminal acts of the degree and the Graduate School assessment template writes them into the outcomes. Bachelor's programs follow, largely through accreditation, while master's programs and certificates fall below them. Undergraduate certificates, the shortest and most directly professional credentials on the list, document communication least, and seven of the 22 have no outcome text on file. Since documentation rises with credential length, the gradient shows where the record is thin, which is a separate question from how much writing and speaking the graduates of those programs do.

Ninety-five outcome strings recur word for word in three or more programs, and three template families, each written outside the department by an accreditor, the Graduate School, or an assessment office, account for most of the recurrence.

The accreditation template

Student Outcome 3 of ABET, the engineering and technology accreditor, "communicate effectively with a range of audiences," appears verbatim in sixteen programs across Engineering, Natural Resources, and the non-departmental engineering programs. With the outcome in place, Engineering documents communication in 40 of its 56 programs and supplies 22 of the university's 68 audience-specific statements. The Graduate School research template does comparable work in eleven graduate programs across five colleges, all of which carry "communicate research effectively in writing" and eight of which also carry "communicate research effectively in oral presentations."

The practice template

Master's programs in the College of Education share a vocabulary of practice, in which "apply what they have learned in field-based practice" and "participate in educational communities relevant to their area of specialization" each appear in 21 programs. Because none of the shared strings names communication, 31 of the college's 39 programs have no outcome naming it, in a college whose graduates write and speak for a living.

The satisfaction template

Graduate certificates carry 205 of the corpus's 337 satisfaction statements, drawn from six reporting items such as "be satisfied with the overall educational experience of the certificate program," and for three certificates those items are the only outcomes on file. Because the items measure satisfaction with the program, the record carries no account of what graduates learned to write or say.

Thinking named, communication assumed

Of the 93 programs with outcome text but no communication outcome, 51 claim analysis, interpretation, description, explanation, synthesis, or critique, which faculty assess through the student papers, reports, and presentations those outcomes leave unnamed. Curricular sites appear as rarely. Capstone occurs in six outcomes across four programs, senior design in four Engineering objectives and no outcome, and portfolio in none of the 2,540 statements. The College of Sciences is the one college whose assessment record names the courses where its communication outcomes are assessed (BIO 424, 426, 460, and 482 through 486 for Biological Sciences, GN 425 for Genetics), because only its reporting form asks where each outcome is assessed.

The non-departmental programs (institutes, interdisciplinary graduate programs, and the Analytics MS) report outside any college, and the Graduate School reports one program of its own, which documents communication. Design names communication in every program because critique and presentation are both its pedagogy and its vocabulary, whereas the Poole College of Management documents it in six of twelve programs, three of them through a single outcome on organizing and expressing ideas, since the standards of AACSB, the business accreditor, keep business communication compact. Humanities and Social Sciences documents communication near the campus rate, with outcomes written program by program in the vocabulary of the disciplines, and seven of the ten programs on campus whose outcomes name argument or rhetoric sit in the college.

NC State among its peers

In September 2026, CWSP re-verified the twelve official peer institutions, approved by the Board of Governors in May 2025, against their current catalogs. Every peer requires writing, and seven run a structured writing-across-the-curriculum (WAC) program with a campus-wide writing-in-the-major or writing-intensive requirement (Rutgers, Texas A&M, Arizona, UC Davis, Illinois, Minnesota, Virginia Tech), while two require a speaking course of every undergraduate (Purdue, Maryland). No peer combines a writing requirement, a structured WAC program, a required speaking course, programs in digital and AI literacy (at NC State, AI Literacies and the AI Policy Lab), and a cross-campus coordinating office (at NC State, CWSP), all five of which NC State has in place. Because no peer publishes its program outcomes as a readable corpus, the documentation findings on this page describe NC State alone.

College summaries

CWSP prepared a one-page summary for each college, reporting its figures by credential level, the outcome language its programs already use, and the programs whose outcomes need one added sentence to record the communication their faculty already assess. The summaries are open to NC State faculty and staff through a shared folder, with an NC State login. Open the college summaries.

Readers outside NC State, or anyone who would like to talk through a summary with CWSP, request one through the contact page.

Working with CWSP

WOLF is the CWSP partnership through which a department, program, or college documents the communication it already teaches, working through the Inquiry, Design, and Evidence phases at its own pace. A department completing the Evidence phase leaves with an outcome statement it wrote itself, an assessment site it named itself, and a rubric it will use again, which together supply the sentence the assessment record is missing. How WOLF works describes the phases, and the contact page opens a first conversation, which carries no commitment. Current programming, including Wolf Wednesdays, sessions of C²TC (the Communication Teaching Certificate), and writing challenges, is listed on the events calendar.
