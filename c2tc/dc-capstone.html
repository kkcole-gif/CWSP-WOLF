Digital Communication Capstone

Your capstone is a digital assignment or instructional module you will use in your own course. Submit it with a rationale for your design choices, your assessment approach, and the way the module puts digital rhetoric and the AI literacy framework to work. Complete the three core modules and one elective before you submit. Plan on 4 to 6 hours.

Part A. Course and context

The course, its level, and where the assignment or module falls in the semester. Describe the digital communication students do in this course now and where they struggle.

Part B. The assignment or module

The full assignment prompt or module materials as students will see them. Your Module 1 analysis assignment, Module 2 project design, or Module 3 operative decision document makes a strong starting point.

Part C. Assessment and accessibility

How you will evaluate the work, including criteria or a rubric, and how the design meets the accessibility practices from the certificate.

Part D. Design rationale

One to two pages on the reasoning behind the design. Which frameworks shaped it, what you decided about AI tools in this assignment and why, and what you expect to be hardest to put into practice.

How CWSP reviews the capstone

CWSP first checks the materials in Part B against WCAG 2.2 AA. A capstone with access barriers in those materials comes back for revision before review begins. A reviewer then rates each part against the description below. A part earns its full weight when it meets the description, half its weight when one element is missing or underdeveloped, and none when it does not yet address the description. CWSP approves a capstone with a weighted total of 80 percent or more. A capstone below 80 percent comes back with comments on the parts to revise, and you resubmit those parts.
