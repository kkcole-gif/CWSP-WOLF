Psychology: AI Literacy and Policy Workbook

For the Psychology working group and its chair. The workbook takes the unit from its filed learning outcomes to written decisions about AI, one outcome at a time. Your baseline and the policy findings are already filled in.

What this workbook is

The Accelerator asks each unit one question per outcome: where students show the outcome, what they do there, what a model now does to the act, and what the program decides about it. The order is the method. An outcome naming the thinking and not the act cannot tell a student's work from a model's, so the silent outcome and the AI question are the same question.

  1. Literacy. Read the three levels (describe, analyze, decide) in your discipline's terms.
  2. Baseline. Check CWSP's reading of your filed outcomes.
  3. Map. For each outcome, name the course, the assessor, the act, and what a model does to it. January sessions.
  4. Policy. Read what peer units and the field have written, and where their language breaks.
  5. Lab. Decide, give the reason, and name the product. February and March sessions.
How saving works

Everything you type stays in this browser. Use Save and email me a link to continue on another computer or to hand the draft to a colleague, who opens it from the link. Anyone holding the link sees the draft. Submit sends the finished workbook to CWSP and emails you a copy. The unit's Google Sheet worksheet (opens in a new window) stays the record of decisions. The spreadsheet download at the end carries your rows into it.

Who you are

Part 1 · LiteracyThree levels, in Psychology

Each row of the worksheet runs three levels in order. CWSP's reading of each level in your discipline sits below its prompt. Correct the reading where it misdescribes the field.

Describe

Psychology studies the cognition a model imitates and runs the empirical method a model now enters at every stage. A large language model predicts likely text from a corpus. In the department's outcomes, the acts it performs on the surface are the graduate research acts (proposing a question, reviewing the literature, planning collection and analysis, critiquing a study) and, for the BA, explaining scientific results to an audience. Describing the system in the unit's terms means naming which of these acts a model performs and which it only appears to perform.

List what students write, say, build, or present in the program. These become column E.

Analyze

The structural questions come from the discipline's own commitments: research ethics and participant confidentiality (what happens to data entered into a tool), the validity of a generated citation or summary, bias in systems trained on unrepresentative text, and the APA's disclosure rules for authors. For students in school psychology and other applied concentrations, the same questions reach clients and schools through the APA's 2025 ethical guidance for practice and the guidance of NASP.

Decide

The operative question is which acts are the judgment the degree certifies. The baseline reading locates it in BA outcome 1 and in graduate outcomes 1, 2, 3, and 5, none of which names where a student performs it. Refusal with a reason is a decision. A decision without a reason is unfinished.

Name two or three. The Lab returns to these.

CWSP's reading

Psychology BA. Documented on one of five outcomes. Outcome 2 names the act (explaining and describing scientific results to an audience in professional style) and is the only outcome naming a piece of writing or a presentation in which a student shows it. Outcome 1 holds the judgment the degree certifies (critical and creative thinking, skeptical inquiry, the scientific approach) without naming where a student performs it, so the outcome a model most affects and the outcome the record documents are different outcomes. Outcomes 3 through 5 name knowledge, professional readiness, and ethical standards.

Psychology MS and PhD. Documented on one of six outcomes. The MS and PhD file the same six statements. Outcome 6 names professional communication in the genre with its style guidelines, which in psychology means APA style and the empirical report. Outcomes 1 through 5 name research acts (propose questions, review the literature, plan collection and analysis, apply a critical perspective to one's own and others' research) without a communicative code, and these are the acts a current model performs most readily, the literature review above all. Outcome 2 is where the Lab's question is sharpest for this program. Documented on one of six outcomes for the PhD, on the same record. For the doctorate the question of which outcomes carry the degree's judgment turns on outcomes 1, 3, and 5, since the dissertation is the site where a student proposes, plans, and critiques, and the record names none of those sites.

Part 4 · PolicyWhat peers and the field say

The run read the unit-level record in psychology departments at NC State's twelve official peers, then the American Psychological Association and its divisions, the National Association of School Psychologists, and the nearest department policies outside the peer set. Each entry gives what the document governs and CWSP's reading of where its language breaks down. The readings are offered for the group to correct.

Finding. Psychology has the most developed disciplinary rule of the three Accelerator units, and it is a rule for authors. The APA's journal policy permits AI with disclosure in the methods section, requires authors to verify what a tool supplies, and bars editors and reviewers from entering manuscripts into a model. Peer departments have extended the publication rule to students rather than writing a rule for learning. Maryland's interim graduate policy and Georgia's guidelines both send students to the APA policy, both require disclosure, and both leave the decision to advisers, chairs, and instructors, with one fixed point at Maryland: first drafts of theses and dissertations are written without AI. Outside the peer set, Buffalo forbids AI for text, outlines, presentation scripts, and statistical analysis in graduate milestones. The undergraduate program has the least guidance anywhere in the record. NC State Psychology would decide whether the BA follows the author's rule of disclosure or a learner's rule about which acts a student performs unaided.

University of Maryland

Department of Psychology · NC State peer, land-grant

Document
INTERIM Policy on Generative/Agentic AI Usage for Graduate Students (opens in a new window), Last updated April 29, 2026
Governs
Graduate coursework, research, TA work, comprehensive exams, theses and dissertations
What it says
Seven general rules: discuss AI at the start of every project, disclose AI-generated text, code, materials, images, and analysis (tool, version, prompts, and output), protect others' privacy, check every output, write first drafts of theses and dissertations without AI, let program areas set exam rules, and follow each supervisor's added rules. Checklists for each context leave the permitted and prohibited columns for supervisors to fill. Grammar checkers are not counted as generative AI.
Where the language breaks
The clinical students' paragraph sends them to the clinic's AI policy with the words INSERT HERE where the reference belongs, so the students working with clients, where confidentiality matters most, are pointed to a policy the document has not located. The checklists are blank by design, which makes the policy a procedure for deciding rather than a decision.

University of Georgia

Department of Psychology · NC State peer, land-grant

Document
Guidelines on the Use of AI (opens in a new window), October 4, 2024
Governs
Coursework, theses and dissertations, instructors' course rules, publishing
What it says
Assembles Graduate School, teaching center, library, and APA rules. Generative AI in a thesis or dissertation counts as unauthorized assistance unless the advisory committee approves it, within the approved scope, with a statement in the document. In coursework, no AI unless the instructor approves it before submission. Manuscripts and others' materials stay out of AI tools.
Where the language breaks
The department's guidance is a compilation of other offices' rules, so the reasons a psychology program would give appear only where the APA's publishing rules are quoted, and the undergraduate student meets a default prohibition without a disciplinary reason.

University at Buffalo

Department of Psychology · Outside the peer set

Document
AI Use in Graduate Milestone Projects (opens in a new window), Adopted March 2026
Governs
Second- and third-year projects, theses, dissertations, qualifying projects, comprehensive exams
What it says
States the goal of graduate training as independent scientists creating new knowledge. Forbids generative AI for text creation, outlines, scripts for proposal and defense presentations, and statistical analysis in milestone projects. Cautions against uploading data. Stony Brook University

Department of Psychology · Outside the peer set

Document
Psychology Department Generative AI Policy (opens in a new window), July 2, 2025
Governs
Exams, academic milestones, course assignments, publication
What it says
AI is barred from exams, milestones, and assignments unless an instructor permits it. Readability edits are allowed with acknowledgment. American Psychological Association

APA Journals · Disciplinary society

Document
APA Journals Policy on Generative AI: Additional Guidance (opens in a new window), Current page, checked November 10, 2026
Governs
Authors, editors, and reviewers of APA journals
What it says
Authors are permitted to use AI with disclosure and are responsible for verifying its output against original sources. Attribution is required when a tool generates ideas, content, analysis, code, or research elements. Confidential material, such as identifiable data or client notes, stays out of open tools. Editors and reviewers are barred from entering submitted manuscripts into AI. Authors are advised to keep prompts and output for their records.
Where the language breaks
The policy governs publication, and the departments in this run carry it into coursework, where a student is not an author submitting a finished study but a learner whose acts the course is meant to train.

How to read a policy for where its language breaks

A governance document written under institutional pressure says more than its authors intended. Look for hedging (strongly discouraged), contradictions between sections or between two documents on one campus, silences where governance should be explicit, frameworks borrowed beyond their scope, and dates (language accurate when written and no longer true). The full method and the AI Governance Census are in the AI Policy Workbook (opens in a new window).

Products

Each decision becomes one of four products. Insert a frame, then rewrite it in your program's words.

Five principles for durable policy language
  1. Scope terms by function. Define AI by what it does (generates text, produces images, automates tasks). Product names date quickly.
  2. Date the policy. State the term the policy covers and commit to revision.
  3. Name the revision trigger. Say what would prompt an update: new tools, new institutional guidance, or evidence of the policy producing the wrong outcomes.
  4. Separate principle from prohibition. State the commitment (what the program develops and assesses) apart from the rule (which functions are barred this term). The first outlasts the second.
  5. Give the reason. A policy with its reason is easier to follow, and a later reviewer knows what to keep when the tools change.

Revised outcome

For an outcome naming only the thinking. Name the act, the object, the audience, and the student's accountability for AI decisions.

Assignment-level AI policy

One policy per assignment, with its reason. A general clause in the syllabus does not do this work.

Rubric criterion for reasoning about use

Grades the reasoning, not the choice. Refusal with reasons meets the criteria.

Program philosophy of use

The program-level account of decisions, one page. The Lab's written record.
