About WOLF

Written and Oral Literacy Facilitation, a faculty development model for building writing and speaking into the curriculum.

What WOLF is

WOLF is a structured faculty development program run by the NC State Campus Writing and Speaking Program (CWSP). It pairs faculty with a trained CWSP liaison for three phases of work. Together they audit how a course teaches communication now, design or revise assignments with deliberate scaffolding, and build evidence of student learning over time.

A WOLF engagement usually runs across a semester or an academic year. It produces concrete materials: revised assignments, rubrics, records of instructional decisions, and program-level data on communication outcomes.

What liaisons and faculty each contribute

The work is collaborative, and faculty direct it. Liaisons bring frameworks for communication pedagogy. Faculty bring their disciplinary expertise, including detailed knowledge of what communication in their field looks like and what it demands of students. Faculty decide how to teach their content. Liaisons work with them on assignments, rubrics, and course design, while editing and responding to student papers stays with the instructor.

The program adapts to the communication contexts of each field, including scientific writing, technical documentation, oral argumentation, professional correspondence, and multimodal composition.

The three-phase model

Phase 01

Inquiry

Map how a course or program teaches communication now. What do students write or say? How does the instructor explain, model, and evaluate those tasks? Inquiry identifies strengths and gaps, and it often finds implicit expectations that students must decode on their own.

Phase 02

Design

Build or revise assignments so they are scaffolded, with instruction, low-stakes practice, and feedback sequenced before a high-stakes submission. In this phase you draft assignment sheets and build rubrics aligned to disciplinary criteria. You also prepare supporting materials that students use throughout the writing or speaking process.

Phase 03

Evidence

Collect and analyze student work to document what changed after the redesign. Faculty use this evidence to keep improving the course. It also feeds program-level assessment data at the university. In this phase faculty make visible the communication learning already under way in their courses.

Who facilitates WOLF

CWSP liaisons facilitate WOLF engagements. Liaisons are faculty, graduate students, or staff who have completed CWSP training in communication pedagogy. They work across disciplines, and CWSP matches each liaison to faculty by disciplinary context and availability.

The CWSP Liaison Guide, WSCP Working Template, and supplementary resources for liaisons are available on the For Liaisons page.

Who WOLF serves

WOLF serves NC State faculty who teach writing- or speaking-intensive courses, programs revising their curriculum, departments in program review, and any instructional unit that wants clearer, more equitable communication expectations in its curriculum.

First-time participants are welcome. Engagements are renewable. Faculty who complete one often return to extend the work or carry it into more courses.
