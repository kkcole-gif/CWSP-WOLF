Research and Presentations

The scholarship and talks behind the CWSP programs on this site.

Publications

Cole, K. K. (2026). AI literacy is not one thing: Conceptual, structural, and operative levels for higher education governance. Pedagogies: An International Journal, forthcoming. The framework page sets out the three levels, runs them on one decision, and offers a decision tool by audience.

Cole, K. K. (in progress). The Glitch Curriculum: How universities read themselves in the AI moment [Book manuscript]. The book reads institutional responses to generative AI as rhetoric, drawing on more than eighty governance documents. It develops glitch as a method for reading institutional texts, and strategic abstraction as a name for governance which disavows its own political work.

Anson, C. M., & Cole, K. (2026). Generative AI does all the work for the writer. Writing with AI requires human rhetorical agency. In Basgier, Mills, Olejnik, Rodak, & Sharma (Eds.), Bad ideas about AI and writing (Chapter 15). WAC Clearinghouse; University Press of Colorado. https://doi.org/10.37514/PER-B.2026.2777.2.15

Association for Writing Across the Curriculum (2025). Statement on Artificial Intelligence Writing Tools. Kirsti Cole is a co-author.

Presentations

The Unmediated Writer: What writing programs do when cognition is distributed. Carolinas WPA 19th Fall Retreat, Wildacres, Session C.1, October 6, 2026. The session page holds the slide deck, the companion document, the evidence, and the works cited.

Research behind the programs

The AI Policy Lab reads a corpus of 80 AI governance documents from 24 institutions, produced between 2023 and 2026. The policy repository workbook annotates the corpus and collects new documents for it.

The AI Literacies extension and the C²TC certificate build on the three-level framework. WOLF follows the University of Minnesota's Writing-Enriched Curriculum model, described on the About page.
